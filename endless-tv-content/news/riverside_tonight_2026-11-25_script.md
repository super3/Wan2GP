## 1. Open and headlines

**1 · 0:00** Good evening. I'm Dana Reyes, and this is Riverside Tonight for Wednesday, November twenty-fifth. On the eve of Thanksgiving, tensions in the Gulf are rising again, and Washington is proposing a new wave of tariffs.

**2 · 0:15** Also tonight: a federal jury convicts Chinese tech giant Huawei, Nvidia brings back the chief executive who resigned just last week, and millions of Americans are on the move for the holiday, with storms on both coasts.

**3 · 0:30** We begin in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow shipping lane between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula that carries a large share of the world's oil. Tonight, Iran says more tankers there have struck mines.

## 2. The Gulf

**4 · 0:45** Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire today, after taking what Tehran calls an unauthorized route through the strait. Iran said yesterday that three other tankers had struck mines, despite an American escort.

**5 · 1:00** Iran has not named the ships or said what happened to their crews, and its claims have not been independently confirmed. Tehran says ships must use Iranian channels, and its army chief has ordered the strait closed further, day by day.

**6 · 1:15** The United States has kept a naval blockade on Iranian ports since July. A third American aircraft carrier, the U.S.S. Theodore Roosevelt, joined the fleet last week, and the Navy says it will destroy any Iranian boat it finds laying mines.

**7 · 1:30** In Washington, the Treasury Department has widened sanctions to more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel. And Qatar's prime minister has been pressing Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President J.D. Vance to restart talks.

**8 · 1:45** Through Qatari mediators, Iran has offered to reopen the strait within forty-eight hours if the United States lifts its blockade and ends the war on all fronts. So far, neither side has signaled any movement on those core demands.

**9 · 2:00** The fighting is spreading across the region. In Saudi Arabia, civil defense officials say eleven people were wounded Tuesday when a strike hit the tallest tower in Riyadh, home to the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund.

**10 · 2:15** It was the second attack on that tower in less than a week, and Yemen's Houthi rebels say they carried it out. Today, the Houthis also claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, saying it started fires.

**11 · 2:30** The Saudi-led coalition says its air defenses shot down four drones over Ras Tanura, but it has not said whether the refinery itself was hit. We'll keep following developments in the Gulf throughout the evening.

## 3. Tariffs

**12 · 2:45** Now to trade. The Trump administration today proposed new tariffs on fourteen economies, including China and the European Union. It follows months of investigations into what Washington calls structural excess capacity: industries producing far more than markets can absorb.

**13 · 3:00** Those investigations began in March and covered sixteen trading partners. The U.S. Trade Representative is proposing a fifteen percent tariff on European Union goods, rising to as much as twenty percent if talks fall short.

**14 · 3:15** Chinese goods would face new tariffs of up to twenty percent, and goods from Vietnam up to thirty-five percent. Japan, South Korea and Taiwan are also on the list. There is no decision yet on Mexico or Norway.

**15 · 3:30** These are proposals, not final rates. Public comments are due January fifth, hearings begin January twenty-fifth, and no start date has been set. Businesses and foreign governments will have their say before anything takes effect.

**16 · 3:45** Beijing pushed back quickly. China's foreign ministry called the claim of overcapacity, quote, a false proposition. The notices also leave open exactly how the new rates would combine with tariffs that are already in place.

**17 · 4:00** For shoppers, none of this arrives in time for the holiday season. But for importers planning for next year, today's announcement sets up a busy winter of comments, hearings and negotiations.

**18 · 4:15** We'll be watching how Europe and Asia respond in the days ahead.

## 4. Huawei

**19 · 4:30** A landmark verdict in Brooklyn. A federal jury has convicted Chinese tech giant Huawei on ten of the twelve criminal counts it faced, in a rare American prosecution of a foreign corporation.

**20 · 4:45** Jurors found the company guilty of bank fraud, wire fraud, violating sanctions on Iran, money laundering and conspiring to steal American trade secrets. Several of its subsidiaries were convicted as well.

**21 · 5:00** But the jury acquitted Huawei of the most sweeping charge, racketeering conspiracy, which prosecutors had used to portray the company as a criminal enterprise. Jurors also cleared it of conspiring to obstruct justice.

**22 · 5:15** The trial lasted about eleven weeks, and the jury deliberated for four days before reaching its verdict on Tuesday. Attorney General Todd Blanche said in a statement that no person or company is above the law.

**23 · 5:30** Huawei's lawyer, Brian Heberlig, said the company is disappointed by the convictions, but that the racketeering acquittal vindicates what he called its good-faith compliance culture.

**24 · 5:45** The case dates back to charges first filed in two thousand nineteen, and it has been a source of friction between Washington and Beijing ever since.

## 5. Nvidia

**25 · 6:00** Now to a dramatic reversal at Nvidia, the chipmaker at the center of the artificial intelligence boom. The company's board has reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, after he resigned last week.

**26 · 6:15** Huang stepped down after reports about his personal relationships raised national security concerns. At the time, the board's chair said directors had accepted his resignation and did not intend to bring him back.

**27 · 6:30** That changed after President Trump got involved. Mr. Trump told CNBC on Monday, quote, I convinced them, and he described Huang as exonerated after what he called a pretty good investigation.

**28 · 6:45** Nvidia says the board's vote was unanimous, and Huang also returns to the board. Colette Kress, who had been serving as interim chief executive, goes back to her role as chief financial officer.

**29 · 7:00** Questions remain. California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office says it will keep investigating Nvidia, and the company says it will continue to cooperate with government investigations and inquiries.

**30 · 7:15** Huang co-founded Nvidia in nineteen ninety-three and has led it ever since. Its chips power much of the artificial intelligence industry, so what happens at the top of the company is watched closely around the world.

## 6. Thanksgiving travel

**31 · 7:30** Finally tonight, if you're heading somewhere for Thanksgiving, you have plenty of company. AAA expects about eighty-one and a half million Americans to travel between yesterday and next Monday.

**32 · 7:45** That's essentially flat from last year, down about a tenth of a percent, ending a three-year streak of record Thanksgiving travel. AAA points to record-high gas prices this fall as one reason fewer people are driving.

**33 · 8:00** Still, almost seventy-three million people are going by car. About six million are flying, and they're paying more. AAA says domestic round-trip fares average around eight hundred dollars, up seventeen percent from last year.

**34 · 8:15** Buses, trains and cruises are the one category growing, with two point six million travelers, up about four percent. The F.A.A. expects four hundred seventeen thousand flights over the nine-day holiday, its busiest Thanksgiving forecast in sixteen years.

**35 · 8:30** Yesterday was expected to be the busiest single day, with about fifty-two thousand flights. It got off to a rocky start at Chicago's O'Hare, where an equipment problem caused a brief ground stop.

**36 · 8:45** Now the bigger worry is the weather. Forecasters are tracking a series of storms crossing the country through Sunday. The Weather Channel expects significant delays tomorrow at all the major Northeast hubs, and snow could slow flights in Saint Louis.

**37 · 9:00** Delays are also possible in Chicago, Detroit, Pittsburgh, Cincinnati and Seattle. If you're flying, check your airline's app before you leave for the airport, and give yourself extra time.

## 7. Recap and close

**38 · 9:15** Once more, tonight's top stories: Iran says more tankers have struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Washington is proposing new tariffs on fourteen economies, and a federal jury has convicted Huawei on ten of twelve counts.

**39 · 9:30** Nvidia has brought back Jensen Huang as chief executive, and more than eighty million Americans are traveling for Thanksgiving, with storms on both coasts. These stories are still developing, and we'll bring you updates as they come in.

**40 · 9:45** That's Riverside Tonight. From all of us here, have a safe and happy Thanksgiving. I'm Dana Reyes. Thank you for watching, and good night.
