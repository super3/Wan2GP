# bold_shapes: style study (late-90s "bold shapes" TV cartoon, UPA-descended)

## Premise
**Squeaky Doyle**, the smallest and bravest window-washer in Sky-High City. He dangles from a bosun's chair a hundred
floors up and treats every pane like a super-mission, using a squeegee twice his size. Everything (character, city, props,
line, music) is original. There are no super-kids, no trio, no lab, no villain and no look-alike locations.

## Traits observed, and how I built each one
1. **Iconic geometric characters.** The head is as big as the body and wider than it is tall. I drew it as a superellipse
   (n=2.35), between an oval and a rounded box, with the features wrapped low on it. The body is a short box, and the stubby
   limbs are drawn as tubes.
2. **Huge eyes, no nose, tiny mouth, no fingers.** The eyes are oversized ovals with one flat iris colour, a black pupil, two
   white catch-lights and a heavy upper-lid stroke. The mouth is a small line and opens into a flat-topped "D" when he talks.
   Hands are round mitten blobs that overlap whatever they hold.
3. **Very thick black outlines.** I ink in two passes, like a cel. Pass one strokes the whole silhouette heavily, then strokes
   it again nudged down-right, so lower contours come out heavier like brush inking. Pass two fills each part and draws its
   lighter interior line. Line widths stay constant under squash and stretch.
4. **Flat candy colours.** The character has no shading at all, only flat fills: yellow hat, tangerine overalls, red boots and
   bucket, blue eyes and pole. Warm, saturated colours on the character sit against a cool background.
5. **UPA-style backgrounds.** The background is flat colour fields with thin lines in a darker shade of each colour, never
   black, so the character pops. It uses a limited mint/teal/lilac palette with one coral accent. Buildings are tidy flat
   shapes with slightly skewed verticals, simple window rows and a face-on lilac facade with flat shine streaks. Clouds have
   flat bottoms and the sun is a plain disc. The background is a cached "painted card" with a faint gouache texture.
6. **Retro-modern 60s flavour.** The skyline has a saucer-topped needle tower, stepped towers, a ribbed dome, an atomic
   starburst mast and a water tank. The music is a spy-lounge vamp in a minor-major-7 key.
7. **Punchy action with speed effects.** For the swipe, the background cuts for 6 frames to a pop-art card (flat violet with
   radiating stripes and white speed streaks). There are two smear frames drawn on ones, where the squeegee's sweep is a single
   flat band, followed by a 4-frame impact shake and four-point twinkles on the clean glass.
8. **Limited-animation timing.** The character animates on twos, with holds, and snaps between strong key poses: rest, blink,
   squashed wind-up, smear, stretched hit, proud "flag" pose with the pole raised, talk, hop with closed happy eyes, settle.
   The final 10 frames return exactly to the key pose, and the bubbles run on a 5 s loop.
9. **Voice and sound.** The voice is formant-synthesised gibberish from a kid, built from 8 syllables. The same syllable list
   drives the mouth flaps. Sound effects include slide-whistle, squeak, chimes, boing and the rope.

## What I'd improve next
- **More line variation.** Taper the strokes at the ends of interior lines, and give hand-inked lines a little boil on held
  frames.
- **Richer background.** Add more background depth, such as a second painted-card layer with a slight parallax hold. Also try a
  candy-pink or lime-sky variant of the palette.
- **Better voice.** The synth voice is cartoon babble. A real performance via the H3 render will carry the line.
- **Squeegee pose.** In the hit pose, the horizontal pole reads a little like a hockey stick. Drawing it on a more diagonal
  pull-down line would read better.
